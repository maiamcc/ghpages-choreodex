english

Maia McCormick (2026)

duple minor longways

the glossary-est dance that ever glossaried. Cast, lead, turn single, set, 2ht

Mairi’s Wedding?? idk trad. 4/4 G major NIB · Sheet music ↗

Choreography

v. easy

A1

  • (1-4) Ones lead down between twos, cast up outside to place (8)
  • (5-8) Ones set to P, turn single R (8)

A1

  • (1-4) Twos lead up between ones, cast down outside to place (8)
  • (5-8) Twos set to P, turn single R (8)
  • (all are home)

B1

  • (1-4) with neighbor, forward a double (towards P) and fall back (8)
  • (and face N up/down)
  • (5-8) set to N and change by R shoulder (8)
  • (all in progressed places)

B2

  • (1-4) Circle left 1x (8)
  • (5-8) 2-hand turn 1x (8)
  • (1s end close, ready to lead down through new 2s)

Background

The tune choice doesn’t really matter?? Any generic marchy 64-count AABB will do!

Teaching Notes

Optionally, setting in A1/A2 may be for everyone (i.e. “all set to partner, turn single R”) instead of just the active couples.

Playford (1701) Interp. Cecil Sharp (1922)

duple minor longways

simple glossary; corners, stars, back to back etc.

2/2 D major BI p. 57 · Sheet music ↗

Choreography

v. easy

A1

  • (1-4) 1st corners set, turn single (8)
  • (5-8) 1st corners two hand turn (8)
  • (all home)

A2

  • (1-8) 2nd corners, the same (16)
  • (all home)

B1

  • (1-4) Star R (8)
  • (5-8) Star L (8)
  • (all home)

B2

  • (1-4) Partners back to back (8)
  • (all home)
  • (5-8) 1s leisurely cast down to second place (8)
  • while 2s lead up (after 4 beats)
  • (new first Cs, acknowledge)

Background

An even easier variation of Heather Webster’s easy B2 modification.

(The original dance B2 has P back to back, and then three changes of a circular hey (PR, NL, PR), on last pass turning back in to face across.)

Teaching Notes

There’s a lottt of time for the cast/lead at the end of B2; consider a beat or two of eye contact for the 1s before they peel back to cast. 2s can wait four counts before leading up.

Playford (1698)

duple minor longways

leading between and casting, set and turn single. Easy peasy.

Hunt the Squirrel 6/8 A major 1709 BI p. 43 · Sheet music ↗

Choreography

v. easy

A1

  • (1-4) 1s lead down between the 2s and cast back to place (8)
  • (5-8) L file dancers lead through the R file dancers and cast back to place (8)

A2

  • (1-4) 2s lead up through the 1s and cast back to place (8)
  • (5-8) R file dancers lead through the L file dancers and cast back to place (8)

B1

  • (1-4) 1L set to corner and turn single R (8)
  • (5-8) 1R set to corner and turn single R (8)

B2

  • (1-4) Circle L half and fall back w/ neighbor (8)
  • (5-8) Partners set and all cross by R (8)

Teaching Notes

B1 may be more intuitive with both 1st Cs setting / both 2nd Cs setting, respectively.

For more recovery time, take out the set at the end of B2; Ps come forward and leisurely cross R (or leisurely 2H turn ½).

Unknown (1790) Interp. Bernard Bentley (1962)

3C

poussettes and R vs. L file practice; dead easy. Slipjig!

9/8 F major BI p. 96 · Sheet music ↗

Choreography

v. easy

A1

  • (1-4) Robins (caller left) CCW lead around standing line (12)
  • (Robin 1 cross above ptnr… hands? skipping?)

A2

  • (1-4) Larks (caller right) CW lead around standing line (12)
  • (Lark 1 cross above ptnr… hands? skipping)

B1

  • Ones “figure 8 poussette”
  • (1-2) (at top) 1s & 2s CW poussette ½ to trade (1st diags fwd) (6)
  • (3-4) (at bottom) 1s & 3s CCW half poussette (1st diags back up) (6)
  • (end: ones at bottom)

B2

  • (1-2) 1s & 3s another half CCW poussette (6)
  • (3-4) 1s & 2s ½ CW poussette (1st diags back up) (6)
  • (end: all home)

C1

  • (1-4) Ones cross & go below (twos move up) (6)
  • (3-4) Ones cross & go below (threes move up) (6)
  • (end: 2, 3, 1)

C2

  • (1-2) Ones lead up to top, cast down to bottom of set (12)
  • (end: 3, 2, 1)

Teaching Notes

For going around the standing line, may be single file or joined hands, walking or skipping or side-step-skipping. Just be clear!

End of B1 into B2: 1s are doing a full CCW poussette around the 3s, it’s just split over the phrase.

Voight (1809) Interp. A. Simons (1961)

duple minor longways

corner geography?? but piece-y, odd momentum changes.

Bishop 2/4 D major 1788 BI p. 65 · Sheet music ↗

Choreography

v. easy

A1

  • (1-4) Circle left (8)
  • (5-6) 1st corners change by R sh (4)
  • (7-8) 2nd corners change by R sh (4)
  • (All halfway around)

A2

  • (1-8) Repeat A1 (16)
  • (All home)

B1

  • (1-4) Ones lead down the middle, turn alone (leisurely) (8)
  • (5-8) Return (zippy, opt. skipping), 1s cast down while 2s move up (8)
  • (All progressed)

B2

  • (1-8) w/ P, full poussette CCW (2nd diagonal (original 1st corners) moving forward) (16)

Teaching Notes

B2. Poussette is CCW, with current first diagonal dancers backing up to start. Another angle on it; current 2nd diags. go forward first, these are the dancers who started as 1st Cs; so if you went first in the A part trades, you go forward in the poussette.

Playford (1701) Interp. Cecil Sharp (1922)

duple minor longways (originally published improper)

siding, full fig 8. Different speeds of 2HT.

2/2 D minor BI p. 20 · Sheet music ↗

Choreography

easy

A1

  • (1-4) Partners side across (L sh, R sh, swirly) (8)
  • (5-8) Partners back to back (8) (face N on side)

A2

  • (1-8) Repeat A1 with neighbor along (16)

B1

  • (1-4) Neighbors two hand turn 1 ½ (opt: skipping), open up across (8)
  • (5-8) Partners two hand turn (8)
  • (All progressed)

B2

  • (1-8) Ones figure eight up through the twos (opt.: skipping) (16)

Teaching Notes

As: Transition from siding into back-to-back is very slick; from back-to-back P -> siding N by L has a momentum change.

B1: Can explicitly note that the two hand turns have different speeds or let dancers figure it out.

B2: Can be a double figure eight for more experienced dancers.

proper duple longways

simple figures but some interesting recombinations, could be good practice for piece-y/odd phrasing.

Judy and Jim’s Wedding Larry Unger 3/4 D major NIB · Sheet music ↗

Choreography

easy

A1

  • (1-3) 1st diags ¾ back to back (R sh) (until L Sh to L Sh) (9)
  • (4) 1st diags R Sh turn single to place (3)
  • (5-8) Ptnr R sh ‘round 1x (12)

A2

  • (1-3) 2nd diags ¾ L Sh back to back (until R Sh to R Sh) (9)
  • (4) 2nd diags L Sh turn single to place (3)
  • (5-8) Ptnr LEFT sh ‘round 1x (12)
  • (all home)

B1

  • (1-4) Circle RIGHT 1x (12)
  • (5-8) Balance the ring, turn single R Sh (12)

B2

  • (1-4) Circle left 1x (12)
  • (5-6) Balance the ring (ones just balance in and not back out?) (6)
  • (7-8) Ones cast down WHILE twos lead up (6)
  • (new first diags acknowledge)

Teaching Notes

Note slightly atypical phrasing in A; may be funky for noodling, check in with musicians.

Playford (1697) Interp. Douglas & Helen Kennedy (1929)

duple minor longways

casting, leading, changing, circling. Hole-in-the-wall cross maybe be a simple lonnng change. PHRASING PRACTICE?

Henry Purcell 3/2 B♭ major 1693 BI p. 52 · Sheet music ↗

Choreography

v. easy

A1

  • (1-4) Ones cast down, lead up center to place (12)

A2

  • (1-4) Twos cast up, lead down center to place (12)

B

  • (1-2) 1st corners change (6)
  • (3-4) 2nd corners, the same (6)
  • (5-6) Circle left halfway (6)
  • (7-8) Ones cast down while twos move up (6)

Teaching Notes

B. Corner crossings here are what is now known as “hole-in-the-wall” style, after this dance. The dancers take a couple steps in, pivot around each other (without hands), and fall back into their corner’s place.

english Video ↗

Charles Bolton (1986)

3C mixer

cute low-stakes mixer

Phalése 2/2 G major 1580 BI p. 5 · Sheet music ↗

Choreography

v. easy

A1

  • PART ONE: UP A DOUBLE
  • (1-4) Partner up a double, fall back (8)
  • (5-8) Face partner, fall back (no hands), come forward 1x (8)

B1

  • CHORUS
  • (1-4) Right diagonals set, trade places Rsh
  • while long 2nd diagonals CCW orbit whole set to change places (skipping) (8) (beginning by crossing straight acr.)
  • (5-6) All turn single RSh (4)

B2

  • (1-2) All set across (facing new partner) (4)
  • (3-6) Partner 2H turn 1 ½ to original side (8)

A1

  • PART TWO: SIDING
  • (1-4) Partner side right (8)
  • (5-8) Partner side left (8)

B1 + B2

  • CHORUS

A1

  • PART THREE: ARMING
  • (1-4) Partner arm right (8)
  • (5-8) Partner arm left (8)

B1 + B2

  • CHORUS

Teaching Notes

Can add a 4th couple, orbiters just have to go further!

Note crooked phrasing; A parts are 8 bars (16 counts), each B part is 6 bars (12 counts) — and not only that, divided into 8 + 4 and then 4 + 8.

Tune: The Minor Spaniard, by Jenny Beer. A minor key variation on “The Spaniard” tune.

Leah’s Waltz

Fried de Metz Herman (1989)

3C (may also be danced as triple minor)

practice waltz time / 3C geography. (Probably slightly harder than the ideal triple minor intro tho.)

Tunes

Title Composer Interp. Time Key Year Source Dots
Amelia’s Bob McQuillen — 3/4 A mixolydian 1981 BII p. 74 ↗
Ashokan Farewell Jay Ungar — 3/4 DM 1982 BII p. 74 ↗

Choreography

v. easy

A1

  • (1-4) Partner set forward, turn single RSh to place (12)
  • (5-8) All circle left ½ into lines (12)

A2

  • (1-4) Partner set forward, turn single RSh to place (12)
  • (5-8) All circle left ½ into lines (now home) (12)

B1

  • (1-2) Ones lead down center (6)
  • (3-4) Ones set right/left facing partner (6)
  • (5-8) Ones lead back to top, cast to bottom, twos & threes lead up (12)

B2

  • (1-4) Bottom four (threes + ones below) star right 1x (12)

Teaching Notes

If dancing as triple minor: signal 3 more with all in. At top: wait out twice. At bottom: dance with ghosts once, wait out once (ie trade).

english Video ↗

Susan Kevra (2017)

3-couple longways

practice swirly siding, meanwhiles, your position in a 3C.

Rachel Bell 2/2 C major 2017 BIII p. 109 · Sheet music ↗

Choreography

v. easy

A1

  • (1-4) Taking inside hands, partners lead up a double and back, turning in toward partner to face down (8)
  • (5-8) Lead down a double and fall back (8)

A2

  • (1-4) Partners swirly siding (over by L, back by R) (8)
  • (5-8) All set to P and turn single R (8)

B1

  • (1-4) Hands 6 circle L ½, drop hands and face partner (8)
  • (5-8) (w/o hands partner) balance F&B, cross by R, turn over R to face partner (8)
  • (3-2-1 proper)

B2

  • (1-4) Top couple (3s) cross by R, go below to middle place (8)
  • while middle couple lead up
  • while bottom couple back to back
  • (5-8) Partners 2-hand turn (middles 1 ½, others 1x)
  • (Ending 2-3-1)

Teaching Notes
